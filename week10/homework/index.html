Week 10 - Time and Oscillation

Assignment

Create an animation with at least two moving shapes - one moving in a repeated motion (e.g. a pendulum or a rotating planet) and the other performing a movement after a predetermined interval of time has passed. You can add more than 2 shapes if you like.

  • Use trigonometric function to describe repeated motion
  • Use time function to calculate the interval
  • De-synchronize the two movements from each other to create complex motion rhythm

Try to use randomness and/or Perlin noise to create organic shapes.

Documentation:

In the process of your work capture the images of the iterations - at least 6 in total. Create a webpage and write a sentence or two for each image explaining the evolution of work. Embed your sketch from openProcessing or P5js to the webpage.

My Process

For this project, I liked the idea of a planet. But I wanted to add layers that moved across one another so that there were clear continents and clouds. I knew this would go beyond my ability to code by hand pretty quickly, but I wanted to see how far I could go, and work with an LLM to fill in gaps, trying to learn from the LLM output as well as the provided class resources within the given timeline.

With this in mind, I appreciated the idea of working in steps. This gave me a way to break the project down into simple steps that I could do myself, followed by LLM-assisted steps, and ultimately full-LLM takeover (with guidance of course).

Step 1

I started with a group of 250 stars. Each star has a random size, position, and brightness to make it look like space.

AI line

Note: From this point forward, I started to use help from AI.

Step 2

Now that I had the stars, I wanted to see if I could add some randomness to their light output over time. I was able to add this twinkle effect by myself.

Step 3

I added a sphere (the planet) in front of the starfield. I also added a radial gradient for shading, a glowing blue drop shadow to show the upper atmosphere, and a blue stroke to show the lower atmosphere.

Step 4

I added continents to the planet. The continents are blobs made with Perlin noise. Each continent sits at a random spot on the sphere of the planet. Each time a frame loads, the sphere "turns" a little bit and the blobs are projected onto the screen with sin/cos, so the land masses move around the globe and then squeeze toward the edges, making them look three-dimensional (this last part was definitely done with LLM assistance).

Step 5

Now that I have continents, I added drifting clouds. I wanted the clouds to move at a different speed from the land masses, and for them to be somewhat transparent so they would be easy to identify as clouds. I also made these using blobs and Perlin noise.

Step 6 [FINAL]

Now that I had the clouds, they looked kind of fake. I worked with Claude to add a time variable to the noise so that they would change shape over time as they travel. Then I added a couple of extra layers to each cloud mass with different transparency per layer to give them a little more dimension.

Note: FULL AI use here. This was just for fun.

Step 7 [BONUS]

Since I was already working with LLM assistance from Claude, I let the AI go wild for a final step. I added a meteor hurtling toward the planet, hitting it, cracks forming, lava spewing, and ultimately the planet exploding into teeny tiny particles, leaving nothing behind. I also added some sound effects and a click event to trigger the animation. This is strictly a bonus step for fun. I mean... I was already 85% there. Let's cause some mayhem while we're at it.
